**CSIR-FORIG \| Forest & Climate Change Division**

**Scientific Knowledge Management System**

**What the System Will Do --- and Why It Matters**

*A plain-language case for adoption at CSIR-FORIG*

Prepared by: **Michael Asubonteng Aning (Josh)** Version 2.0 \| 2026

*This document is the plain-language companion to the system\'s separate
technical blueprint. It is written for decision-makers and explains,
without jargon, exactly what the system would do for the institute and
why each capability is needed.*

1\. The Idea, in Plain Terms

CSIR-FORIG produces valuable scientific knowledge every day --- field
data, project reports, literature reviews, funding proposals, and the
decisions behind them. Today, most of that knowledge lives in scattered
places: personal laptops, email threads, chat messages, external drives,
and individual memories. Within months it becomes hard to find, and when
a researcher moves on, much of it leaves with them.

The proposed system is a single, secure home for the institute\'s
scientific knowledge. In plain terms, it does five things:

-   It keeps every project and its documents in one organised place.

-   It lets scientists see what work is happening across the institute,
    while keeping the contents of each project private until the owner
    chooses to share.

-   It gives every report a clear, traceable path to the Scientific
    Secretary, replacing informal email and chat.

-   It builds a permanent, searchable library so knowledge is not lost
    when people leave.

-   It includes an assistant that searches across everything stored and
    answers questions in plain language --- always pointing to the exact
    document the answer came from.

Adopted institute-wide, it becomes an asset that grows more valuable
every year, because every project, report, and answer added to it
compounds.

2\. Why CSIR-FORIG Needs This

Three problems affect every knowledge-producing institution. They are
well documented in research, and they are visible at FORIG today. The
figures below come from international studies --- they describe a
pattern that applies to any institution relying on personal storage and
informal sharing, which is FORIG\'s current situation.

2.1 Knowledge becomes unreachable as it ages

A study of biological research data tracked the data behind 516
published papers and found that, for every year that passed, the chance
the underlying data could still be located fell by roughly **17
percent**, and the chance of even reaching the original author by email
fell by about **7 percent a year**. The authors concluded plainly that
research data cannot be reliably preserved by individual researchers
over the long term (Vines et al., 2014). A forestry institute that
depends on personal drives and inboxes is exposed to exactly this decay.

2.2 Staff time is lost hunting for information

The McKinsey Global Institute estimated that knowledge workers spend
close to **one full working day every week --- about a fifth of their
time** --- simply searching for and gathering the information they need
to do their jobs (McKinsey Global Institute, 2012). A separate survey of
982 knowledge workers found the average person spends only about **30 of
40 weekly hours** on productive work, with information-hunting a leading
drain (APQC, 2021). Every hour a FORIG scientist spends searching for a
past report or a colleague\'s dataset is an hour not spent on science.

2.3 When people leave, the institute forgets

A 2018 workplace-knowledge study estimated that about **42 percent** of
an organisation\'s institutional knowledge sits only in the heads of
individual employees --- undocumented anywhere --- and therefore walks
out the door when they do (Panopto, 2018). For a research institute that
hosts national-service personnel and students and sees staff rotate,
this is a recurring, silent loss.

**A note on these figures.** *They come from international studies, not
from a measurement taken at FORIG. They are included to show the size of
a problem the institute shares, not to claim a precise local number. One
useful by-product of the system is that, once running, it would let
FORIG measure its own figures for the first time.*

3\. What the System Will Do

Each capability below answers a real, present-day frustration.

3.1 One organised home for every project

Every project gets a single record holding what it is, who leads it,
which division it sits in, where the funding comes from, its start and
end dates, its research theme and location, its status (proposed,
active, completed, or archived), and all its attached files --- data
sheets, photos, maps, and importantly the documented receipts and
supporting paperwork that today get separated from the project they
belong to. A project no longer lives in five different places at once.

3.2 Reports that follow a proper path

Quarterly reports, mid-year reports, and end-of-year reports are created
inside the system and submitted to the Scientific Secretary through a
formal, traceable channel --- not as an email attachment or a chat
message. When a scientist submits, the system records who submitted,
when, and exactly which version. The Secretary can approve, return with
comments, or escalate, and every step is time-stamped and kept. Both
sides always know a report\'s status. This replaces the most common way
reports are lost today: buried in an inbox.

3.3 A record of papers and student research

The system tracks the institute\'s scholarly output --- papers that are
published and those still in progress --- and the research carried out
by CCST students. The institute can therefore answer, at any moment,
what it has produced, what is in the pipeline, and which student
projects are underway, instead of reconstructing it from memory each
reporting season.

3.4 See what exists, without exposing what is private

Every scientist can see a directory of the projects happening across
FORIG --- the title, the lead, the division, the theme, and the status.
But the contents of a project stay closed unless the owner grants
access. This does two things at once: it stops two teams unknowingly
duplicating the same work, and it helps people discover who to
collaborate with --- while a scientist\'s unpublished data and ideas
remain fully their own until they choose to share. Importantly, the
private parts are never sent to another person\'s screen; the protection
is real, not just a hidden button.

3.5 An assistant that finds answers and shows its sources

A scientist can ask a plain-language question --- a topic, a research
question, or a few keywords --- and the assistant searches across
everything stored and pulls together what is relevant. Every statement
it gives points back to the exact document it came from, so the
scientist can open the source and check it. It is deliberately built to
say "the library does not contain enough to answer this" rather than
guess. This turns years of accumulated reports into something a person
can actually query in seconds.

3.6 A permanent institutional library

Approved reports flow automatically into a searchable library, and older
material --- past reports, field notes, legacy literature --- can be
added too. This is the long-term memory the institute currently lacks: a
place where knowledge is preserved and findable years later, rather than
decaying on individual drives --- the very decay measured in Section
2.1.

3.7 A built-in way to share with colleagues

The system includes its own messaging and sharing, so a scientist can
pass a dataset or document to a colleague inside the secure environment
instead of relying on personal email or chat apps where things get lost.
Sharing happens where the data already lives.

3.8 It keeps working when the internet does not

Core functions are built to work on weak or absent connectivity and to
sync once the connection returns --- important for field stations and
for real network conditions in Ghana. (The assistant in 3.5 needs a
connection to do its synthesis; the system makes that clear when
offline.)

3.9 Security and privacy are enforced by the system, not by trust

Who can see and do what is decided by the system itself, based on each
person\'s role --- scientist, senior researcher, division head,
Scientific Secretary, or administrator. A scientist\'s restricted
project cannot be opened by someone without permission, even by
accident. And the institute\'s knowledge base is backed up regularly to
a separate location, so a single failure can never erase it.

5\. A Day in Practice

A scientist starting a new study on a tree species asks the assistant
what FORIG has already done on it. In seconds it lists three past
projects and two reports, each linked to its source. From the project
directory she sees that a colleague in another division ran a related
survey two years ago; she requests access and avoids repeating fieldwork
that has already been done. At the quarter\'s end she submits her
progress report through the system; the Scientific Secretary sees it
immediately, with the version and timestamp recorded. Three years later,
after she has moved on, a new researcher finds all of it intact in the
library.

7\. What the System Will Not Do

Setting honest limits is part of earning trust in the system\'s outputs.

-   The assistant is not a general internet search engine and not a
    substitute for literature review. It draws only on FORIG\'s own
    stored knowledge. This must be made clear so no one builds a
    proposal on an answer it could not properly source.

-   The assistant is only as good as what is put into the library. A
    well-kept repository makes it genuinely useful; a neglected one
    makes it unreliable. The system rewards discipline.

-   The assistant needs an internet connection. Offline, scientists can
    still browse and search the library, save their questions, and
    receive synthesised answers once they reconnect.

-   The system does not replace human judgement, peer review, or the
    Scientific Secretary\'s authority. It supports those; it does not
    override them.

8\. How It Would Be Introduced

Rather than switching the whole institute over at once, the system would
be introduced in stages, so problems are found while they are still
small:

-   Start with a small group of willing scientists in one division.

-   Expand to the full division and the Scientific Secretary.

-   Open it institute-wide, with support available.

-   Move to full adoption, watching the early months closely and
    adjusting.

This staged approach keeps risk low and lets the institute shape the
system around how people actually work.

9\. The Bottom Line

The value of this system is not what it does on the first day. It is
what it accumulates. Every project filed, every report submitted, every
question answered makes the institute\'s shared memory deeper and more
useful --- a compounding asset that outlasts any individual researcher
and that, unlike personal drives and inboxes, does not quietly decay.
Built and adopted properly, it becomes one of CSIR-FORIG\'s most
valuable long-term assets.
